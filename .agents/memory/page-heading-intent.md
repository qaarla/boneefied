name: Page heading placement
description: Owner's centered heading treatment and Dynamic Island clearance requirement.
---

Center page headings, section headings, system subtitles, module counts, and lesson heading/subtitle blocks. Remove the small uppercase system-code label above module titles (the owner's example was “CELL”). Keep reading paragraphs, anatomical annotations, and lists in their existing reading alignment.

**Why:** The owner supplied a dark-mode iPhone screenshot of Cells & Tissues with the header too close to the Dynamic Island and requested moving it down, removing the top-left CELL label, and centering headers/subheaders “for everything.”

**How to apply:** Preserve the real window's top safe area across headerless nested navigators, then add a comfortable reading gap. Do not treat a nested navigator's zero local top inset as proof that the device has no notch. Keep the persistent dock and atlas geometry unchanged. Browser screenshots do not establish physical iPhone clearance.

Centering alone does not satisfy the placement requirement: headings and subtitles must have visible breathing room below the Dynamic Island.

**Why:** The owner supplied another framed preview showing Muscular System still overlapped by the island after centering, and specifically asked whether the downloaded app would look cramped too. A desktop browser inside a phone frame can report zero hardware safe-area inset even while the surrounding frame draws an island.

**How to apply:** Verify that framed phone previews reserve notch space separately from the reading gap. Keep simulated preview measurements distinct from real native insets, and never claim physical-device verification from a web screenshot.

Do not reposition the home/brand header as a side effect of fixing a system heading.

**Why:** The owner identified the lowered home header as an unauthorized change after a shared spacing adjustment affected it.

**How to apply:** Scope system-heading clearance adjustments to the relevant pages. The home header retains the prior downloaded appearance. Subsequent explicit approval to restyle the dock does not authorize another home-header or app-wide spacing change.

The home brand header must be one compact horizontal row at enlarged text settings too: existing front-facing skull/atom logo on the left, full “Boneefied” title centered between balanced side spaces, and the existing Settings gear on the right. Local title fitting is acceptable; globally disabling accessibility text scaling is not.

**Why:** The owner reported that the installed iPhone stacked the title below the logo and gear despite the approved preview showing the intended row, and expressly requested a header-only native layout correction.

**How to apply:** Check native row geometry and phone-width text fitting rather than treating a web screenshot as native proof. Preserve the artwork, Tinos, Settings navigation/tap target, safe-area clearance, background and all content outside this header.

Center “Learning modules” independently and remove only its adjacent “18 available” display counter. Retain all 18 published learning modules and their existing order, cards, content, progress, bookmarks, art, search and navigation. Module cards stay on the normal page scroll, never another bounded/nested scroller.

**Why:** The owner explicitly clarified that “18 AVAILABLE” means the redundant heading label, not the 18 catalog items, and requested this as a separate presentation-only correction.

**How to apply:** Preserve category-heading measurement and filtering behavior, the previously corrected Explore heading and all system choices.

Remove the orange “Browse glossary →” shortcut from the Study home screen and close the space occupied by its row. Do not add a replacement home control.

**Why:** The owner requested presentation/navigation cleanup only, explicitly protecting the glossary itself, its terms and definitions, typo-tolerant search, aliases, canonical mappings, search suggestions and internal/deep-link destinations.

**How to apply:** Keep glossary access through existing search-related navigation and internal routes; preserve the order, typography and scrolling behavior of the other home sections.
