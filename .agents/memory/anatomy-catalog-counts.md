---
name: Anatomy catalog counts
description: How to assess content completeness when composed content differs from authored module exports
---

Treat the assembled runtime catalog as the source of truth for content totals and lesson-level question coverage, rather than summing authored module exports or trusting a stored manifest alone.

**Why:** Composition adds visual/practical expansions and later lessons/questions after initial module definitions; a stored manifest once understated even the number of lessons in a published module. An apparently empty or thin area may be populated at runtime, while individual cards can still lack related questions.

**How to apply:** For future content audits, calculate counts and question-to-lesson structure overlap from the final assembled catalog; compare the manifest to that result and test displayed selector routes against the same catalog.

For anatomical endpoint reviews, also trust the final published coordinates over
the original generator's seed coordinates.

**Why:** A cartilage generator's original lacuna pin was centered on a
chondrocyte, while the assembled published target had already been corrected
into the pale lacunar space. Reapplying the authored seed would undo a valid
correction.

**How to apply:** Inspect the actual raster at the assembled target before
moving an endpoint or regenerating an entire pack. Preserve later endpoint and
overlay corrections when regenerating source artwork.

Full-legend verification must use the plate's owning module/lesson viewer,
not only a focused glossary card reached from another module.

**Why:** A focused glossary intentionally showed a single selected target and
made other annotations appear unavailable. The full lesson viewer exposed the
same plate's complete markers and legend without any product change.

**How to apply:** Follow published lesson uses from the assembled inventory
before reporting missing labels or incomplete interactive coverage. Keep a
focused glossary check distinct from a full-plate legend check.
